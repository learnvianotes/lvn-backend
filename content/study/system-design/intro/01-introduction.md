A complete reference built from your course notes, whiteboard sessions, and case study images. Every concept defined. Every trade-off explained. Every case study dissected.

> [!NOTE] Three Levels of Design
>
> <strong>High-Level Design (HLD):</strong> How systems interact — servers, databases, APIs, protocols, data flow.<br><br><strong>Low-Level Design (LLD):</strong> Classes, functions, data structures, algorithms, code-level decisions.<br><br><strong>System Principles:</strong> Scalability, fault tolerance, consistency, availability — the why behind every decision.

> [!NOTE] What Makes a Good System
>
> A production-grade system must be:<br><br>• <strong>Scalable</strong> — handles growth without degrading<br>• <strong>Fault tolerant</strong> — survives component failure<br>• <strong>Extensible</strong> — easy to add features without rewrites<br>• <strong>Observable</strong> — logs, metrics, tracing, alerting<br>• <strong>Decoupled</strong> — services don't know each other's internals

# Suggested Study Path

Scaling → Load Balancing → Caching → Sharding → Microservices → Case Studies

> [!NOTE]
>
> <strong>Interview Framework:</strong> For every system, practice: (1) clarify requirements, (2) estimate scale, (3) HLD sketch, (4) deep dive on the bottleneck, (5) discuss trade-offs. That's the exact structure top candidates use.
